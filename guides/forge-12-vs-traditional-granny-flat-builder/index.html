Buyer's Guide · Comparison

Factory-built vs a traditional
on-site granny flat build

Both can end up NCC Class 1A certified. The real differences are in timeline, price certainty, and what happens when something goes wrong mid-build — an honest comparison, not a sales pitch against builders doing it the traditional way.

Both routes can get you a real, certified home

This isn't a comparison between "real building" and "fake building" — a well-run traditional builder delivers a genuinely certified NCC Class 1A dwelling, same as a factory-built one. The honest differences are in how you get there: what's fixed before you commit, how much the timeline depends on things outside anyone's control, and how variations get handled when they come up.

Timeline: sequential vs parallel

A traditional on-site build happens sequentially on your block: slab, then frame, then lock-up, then fit-out, with each trade waiting on the one before it — and every stage exposed to weather delays, which are a genuine factor across Queensland's wet season. A factory build happens indoors, under controlled conditions, in parallel with site preparation (slab, services connections) happening on your block at the same time. That parallel timeline is the single biggest practical difference between the two approaches.

Price: fixed spec vs evolving scope

A fixed-specification factory build has its price locked before construction starts — what you see is what gets built, with delivery and site costs quoted separately and upfront. A custom on-site build gives you more design freedom, but that freedom comes with more opportunities for variations, and variations are where on-site build budgets typically move. Neither approach is wrong — it depends whether you value price certainty or design flexibility more for your project.

Which one actually suits you

If your priority is a specific, non-standard design and you're comfortable managing variations, a traditional builder gives you more room to get there. If your priority is knowing the price and the delivery date before you commit, a certified factory-built home like the Forge 12 is built for exactly that. Either way, the one non-negotiable is the same: confirm NCC Class 1A certification before you sign anything, whichever route you choose.

FAQ

Common questions

Is a modular granny flat cheaper than a traditional on-site build?

Not necessarily cheaper on the display price alone — the real difference is price certainty. A factory-built home has a fixed specification and price before construction starts, whereas an on-site build is more exposed to weather delays, trade availability and variation costs that add up during the project.

Why is a factory-built home faster than a site build?

Because it's built indoors under controlled conditions in parallel with site preparation, rather than sequentially on your block where every trade waits on the one before it and the whole job stops for bad weather.

Does a modular home have the same quality as a site-built house?

When it's certified NCC Class 1A, yes — it's assessed against the same National Construction Code requirements as a site-built house. Factory conditions also reduce the quality variability that comes from different trades and weather on different building sites.

Can I customise a factory-built home the way I could with a traditional builder?

Less than a fully custom on-site build, by design — that's the trade-off for price and timeline certainty. A fixed, well-specified model like the Forge 12 removes the variation costs and delays that come from a highly customised design, in exchange for a smaller set of proven options.
