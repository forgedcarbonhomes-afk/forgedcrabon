Centuries before modern building materials existed, Japanese craftsmen discovered that charring timber with an open flame made it harder, insect-resistant, weather-proof, and built to outlast generations. They called the technique Shou Sugi Ban — and they used it to build temples and palaces that still stand today.

That's the philosophy behind every structure we build. We didn't name our company Forged Carbon by accident. Fire doesn't destroy this timber — it protects it. That's the standard we hold ourselves to on every build across Queensland.

The charring process creates a carbonised outer layer that repels moisture, prevents insect penetration, and resists the UV degradation that destroys conventional cladding within years. The result is a surface that weathers beautifully rather than deteriorating — deepening in character over time rather than fading and cracking.

In Queensland's climate — the UV intensity, the humidity cycles, the termite presence — Shou Sugi Ban isn't just aesthetically compelling. It's the most sensible exterior cladding decision you can make. Japanese builders understood this centuries ago. We think it's time Australia did too.

The Build Standard

The cladding philosophy extends to everything else we build. NCC Class 1A certification isn't a marketing point — it's a baseline commitment to the same structural, electrical, plumbing, and safety standards as any house on any street in Australia.

The Technique

Shou Sugi Ban

Yakisugi — charring the surface of timber using an open flame — creates a carbonised outer layer that is harder, more water-resistant, and more insect-repellent than the raw timber beneath. The technique requires no chemical treatment and no synthetic coatings.

The Performance

Built to Outlast

Properly charred Shou Sugi Ban cladding outperforms painted or stained timber by three to one on lifespan. The carbonised surface resists moisture ingress, UV degradation, and termite activity — critical performance criteria in Queensland's climate.

The Standard

NCC Class 1A

The cladding philosophy is applied to the entire build. NCC Class 1A certification — the same classification as any house on any street — means the Forge 12 is council approvable, insurable, and a genuine appreciating permanent asset.
